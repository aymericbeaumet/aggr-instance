I went to San Francisco for Muni Heritage Weekend 2026! I stood by the tracks for three hours with my [line scan camera](https://daniel.lawrence.lu/blog/2026-06-12-standalone-cnc-line-scan-camera/), scanning trams.

Tram pics are freely licensed under CC-By-SA 4.0 (I’ve also uploaded a bunch to Wikimedia Commons).

## [1](https://daniel.lawrence.lu/blog/2026-09-20-muni-heritage-weekend/#s1) No. 1: Muni Streetcar 1 (1912)

[Muni No. 1](https://www.streetcar.org/streetcars/1-1-muni-1912/) is the first publicly owned big city streetcar in the US. Before then, all streetcars were privately owned. It’s pretty cool!

![Muni streetcar 1](https://i.dllu.net/2026-09-19-21-47-51_NECTA0000_6d669236092eb4c7972e4511d8e63111fe263276_238404890f138d2c.jpg)

## [2](https://daniel.lawrence.lu/blog/2026-09-20-muni-heritage-weekend/#s2) No. 162: Muni (1914-1950s)

[Muni No. 162](https://www.streetcar.org/streetcars/162-162-muni-1950s-livery/) started out from 1914 and served until 1958. It’s making its first Muni Heritage Weekend appearance since a 2014 collision. Sorry, there was a car in the background and someone bumped my camera, causing some vibrations.

## [5](https://daniel.lawrence.lu/blog/2026-09-20-muni-heritage-weekend/#s5) No. 578: Dinky (1896)

[Muni No. 578](https://www.streetcar.org/streetcars/578-578s-msry-dinky/) is probably the oldest one. It looks like a cable car but it is electrically powered rather than being powered by a cable.

Note that up until 1909, Divisadero Street used to be called [Devisadero Street](https://hoodline.com/2014/04/how-devisadero-became-divisadero/), so it’s a period-correct spelling, not a typo.

![Dinky](https://i.dllu.net/2026-09-19-20-49-30_NECTA0000_b8019d7d09e2c746df81bc1bc000e6120f159dc4_96a41af2a9531bdb.jpg)
