It appears that Anthropic has just solved the Riemann Hypothesis, and I have Thoughts. So I’m going to start by telling you about a musical I saw last week.

![](https://i0.wp.com/signoregalilei.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Galileo_Musical_Cover_Art.jpg?w=380&ssl=1)

Given my entire personality and identity, it was basically mandatory for me to see *Galileo* on Broadway. I went to the second day of previews. The musical itself is at least kind of a *Hamilton* knock-off – with a stubborn main character, a friendship that goes wrong, family drama, and of course a historical setting with anachronistically modern tunes (though rock rather than hip-hop). Its greatest strength is its actors, especially Joy Woods as Galileo’s daughter Virginia and Jeremy Kushnier as Galileo’s friend/foil Bishop Barberini. A lot of it seemed to be pandering, but it was pandering exactly to me. I give it an 8.5 out of 10.

![](https://i0.wp.com/signoregalilei.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/Large_1923_0668_0003_.jpg?w=330&ssl=1)

*A replica of Galileo’s telescope [1](https://signoregalilei.com/2026/11/20/and-yet-it-moves/#sdfootnote1sym)*

The best part of this current trend of historical musicals is that it brings across how so many parts of our history are kind of insane, in a way we don’t usually take the time to appreciate. *Hamilton* is the story of a guerrilla uprising seizing control of the largest colony of the world’s most powerful empire, guided by Enlightenment philosophy of all things. *Galileo* is the story of a man literally seeing farther than anyone had before, and upending the nature of truth. We can find out what is true by just asking the natural world in the right way. These ideas are literally revolutionary.

The Riemann Hypothesis has been the crown jewel of unsolved mathematics since Fermat’s Last Theorem was solved in the 1990s. Myriad conjectures have been proven true conditional on the Riemann Hypothesis. That proof instantly unlocks an incredible amount of the universe of math – not unlike the telescope did for our universe.

When I imagined the day the proof was announced, I’d pictured celebrations in nerd circles on par with the biggest I’ve ever seen – Mars rovers touching down, the LHC finding the Higgs boson, and the first picture of a black hole. Dancing in the streets, if you live on a very specific street.

![](https://i0.wp.com/signoregalilei.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/330px-Perseverance_Landing_Skycrane.jpeg?w=330&ssl=1)

*Dare mighty things*

Instead, the mood feels mournful, or bittersweet at best. The proof came from an inscrutable silicon mind, not one of us. Maybe we could have solved this one ourselves, but instead we gave up and checked the answer at the back of the book. Perhaps it’s beautiful and wondrous that the proof exists at all, but it’s not a crowning moment of human achievement. Revolutionary, perhaps, but not in the same way.

Legends tell that after the Inquisition forced Galileo to formally recant his teachings on the Copernican system and the motion of the Earth, he muttered *Eppur si muove –* “And yet, it moves”. Truth is not what others tell us. Truth is just how the world *is*.

We are staring down the end of our role in Galileo’s project. There might still be time for more human discoveries, but the window is closing. Someday soon, any discovery we happen to make ourselves will be because no one bothered to pass the question off to a powerful machine mind. That’s not the world I wanted, but it’s how the world is. It might be selfish and childish, but on days like today, I wish that progress would go no further. I wish that history would stop.

And yet, it moves.
