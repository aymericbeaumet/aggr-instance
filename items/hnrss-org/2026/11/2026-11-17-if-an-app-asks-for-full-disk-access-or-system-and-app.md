Apps that might ask for Full Disk Access or System & App Protection Override include backup apps, disk cleaners, file indexers, security apps, and others. While some of these apps operate best when granted broader access, others may ask for the same level of access even when they don't need it. It's important to understand the risks of granting broad access to any app, so you can make informed decisions about your own data and privacy.

When you give an app Full Disk Access instead of choosing only specific protected folders for it to access, it can bypass system protections and access all of the files on your Mac, including your messages, emails, photos and videos, data from other user accounts, and more. It can read, change, delete, and export this data without asking.

When you give an app System & App Protection Override, you can choose specific apps for it to control. If you allow it to bypass system protections instead, it can fully control your Mac and apps, which allows it to access all of your data. It can also see every window and app, including ones in the background, and capture every keystroke.

An app might ask you to give it access to all of the files on your Mac, or to let it control your Mac. When you turn on access for the app in Privacy & Security settings, depending on the app, your Mac prompts you to:

- Allow or not allow the app to have access. This option appears for some apps, such as backup apps that ask for Full Disk Access, and screen readers and voice control tools that ask for Accessibility.

- Choose specific protected folders or apps for the app to access instead. This option appears for other apps that ask for Full Disk Access or System & App Protection Override. It allows the app to access only what you choose, while system protections stay in place for the rest of your data.

To keep more of your data protected, choose specific protected folders or apps for the app to access instead of bypassing system protections.

When your Mac prompts you, click Choose Folder or Choose Apps, then:

- Check the app's setup instructions for information about which protected folders or apps it needs.

- Choose only the protected folders or apps that it needs.

- For System & App Protection Override, choose whether the app needs to ask for your permission before it controls each app. Use the menu next to each app to choose Ask First or At Any Time.

- If you're not sure which protected folders or apps to choose, [contact the app developer](https://support.apple.com/en-us/102435). To stay protected, wait until you're sure which folders or apps that the app needs before giving access.

You can change which protected folders or apps that the app can access at any time.

Bypassing system protections might expose your personal information to an app that doesn't behave as you expect. An app with Full Disk Access can read, change, delete, and export all of the files on your Mac, and an app with System & App Protection Override can fully control your Mac and apps.

If you're certain that the app is from a trustworthy source and needs this level of access, you can give it Full Disk Access or System & App Protection Override permissions by clicking Bypass System Protections:

- Confirm each action that the app will be able to take:

  - Full Disk Access: Read, Change, Delete, and Export All Your Data; View Hidden and Recently Deleted Photos; and Browse As You on Websites.

  - System & App Protection Override: Read, Change, Delete, and Export All Your Data; View Hidden and Recently Deleted Photos; Browse As You on Websites; Control Your Mac, Apps, and Settings; and Monitor Everything You Do.
- If you're absolutely sure that you want to give the app Full Disk Access or System & App Protection Override, click Turn On Full Disk Access or Allow System & App Protection Override, then enter your password and click Modify Settings.

You can review app permissions in Privacy & Security settings, and change or remove them at any time.

1. From the Apple menu, choose System Settings.

2. Click Privacy & Security in the sidebar, then click Files & Folders, Full Disk Access, or System & App Protection Override.

3. Change an app's access:

   - Remove an app's access: Select the app, then click the Remove button (−). If asked, enter your password, then click Modify Settings.

   - Change which protected folders or apps an app can access: In Files & Folders or System & App Protection Override, click the Info button (i) next to the app, make your changes, then click Done.

   - Limit an app with Full Disk Access or System & App Protection Override to specific protected folders or apps: Remove the app's access, then click the Add button (+) to add the app again. If your Mac prompts you, choose specific folders or apps.

If you remove an app's access, the app might not work as expected.
