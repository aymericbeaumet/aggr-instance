From Roseate Spoonbills in the Grassrivers to gulls over Vice Beach, here's what it takes to find Leonida’s birds, how the spotting mechanics work, and what Rockstar’s artists got right.

I finished collecting the bricks of cocaine and landed my bullet-ridden seaplane at the dock. It was time for birdwatching.

A few minutes after midnight on Thursday morning, I joined millions of other gamers for the release of Grand Theft Auto VI, the latest in the smash video game series from Rockstar Games. The new installment is already a worldwide sensation, earning more than $1.8 billion in revenue the day it was released. 

Grand Theft Auto, or GTA, games are famous for their “open worlds,” which allow players to explore massive, intricately detailed versions of America. Much of that exploration is focused on mayhem and chaos: The story missions usher players through criminal underworlds, and players are at any time free to steal cars, rob stores and pedestrians, and initiate anarchy. 

But GTA 6 has more going on than just criminality—it’s also a world filled with wildlife. Previous GTA games were set in fictionalized versions of New York City and Hollywood, but the new one returns to Vice City, its version of Miami, which was also the setting for 2002’s installment. Unlike that game, however, GTA 6’s South Florida is complete with dozens of species of birds, reptiles, amphibians, fish, mammals, and other creatures. The in-game Progress menu tracks all kinds of items to find, including 338 vehicles, 205 weapons, and, of primary interest to me, at least 60 bird species. 

I made it my mission to find them all as quickly as possible.

(Side note: Pre-release marketing for GTA 6 touted 62 bird species, but the official birds Progress screen only lists 60, with a handful of others listed on the hunting screen.)

You begin the game as Jason, a low-level criminal working to raise money to support his young family. You’re equipped with zoom binoculars before the very first mission—tracking drug runners through a swamp and escaping with the seaplane—and I used them immediately, stopping mid-lurk to scan some Double-crested Cormorants on a dock. (Distracted from the job at hand to look at birds—just like real life!) They looked like Double-crested Cormorants! 

After the first mission the player is free to explore the entirety of the map, stealing cars, robbing stores, or just birding wherever they want. The game makes birding easy: Looking at any bird through the in-game binoculars gives players the option to hold a button to “study” it, thereby adding it to the Progress menu. Simply standing on the back deck of Jason’s first safehouse yields a bunch of species like Brown Pelican and Laughing Gull. It’s just like being in South Florida!

I found a few more species right in Jason’s backyard, including American Crow, House Sparrow, and South Florida-appropriate Monk Parakeets.

Looking south from the back deck, I spotted another bird perched on an adjacent dock, with a few people gathered at the end. Looking through my binoculars I spotted my first Herring Gull—and noticed that the people, a woman and three men, were fully and visibly naked. Jason apparently lives next to a nudist resort. It’s just like being in South Florida!

Two down, 58 to go. I found a few more species right in Jason’s backyard, including American Crow, House Sparrow, and South Florida-appropriate Monk Parakeets, before venturing out into the huge map. My strategy for finding birds in GTA 6 was the same as finding birds in real life: look in as many habitats as I could find. I started by “borrowing” a fanboat off a nearby dock and heading back to the swampy areas near the seaplane mission. There were tons of birds across the open water and in the marsh: Caspian Terns, Mottled Ducks, Sanderlings, Spotted Sandpipers, Great Blue Herons, Glossy and White Ibis, and … flocks of Whooping Cranes?

In fact, I found a number of avian inaccuracies in just my first few hours of playtime. Certain species like Whooping Crane and Red-cockaded Woodpecker are common throughout the map, despite being endangered and threatened, respectively, in real life. Golden Eagles are extremely rare in Florida, but I found one soaring over the Mt. Kalaga area (mountains, one may point out, aren’t found in Florida either). Some species have confusing naming quirks—the chickadee is Black-capped instead of Carolina, and there’s a “New World Oriole” instead of a Baltimore Oriole—but those changes are likely due to the fact that Carolina and Baltimore don’t exist in the GTA universe.


Though some of the inaccuracies may feel like a letdown in a game that is being hailed as the Greatest of All Time, I think it’s important to remember that it’s also game where you can get a ride from a dog driving a jet ski. Grand Theft Auto games are meant to be fun, not science. Plus, despite some of the misfires, the avifauna feels well thought-out. The map is true to the ecological boundaries of South Florida, with herons, gulls, and other waterbirds in the south while birds like Red-bellied Woodpecker, Wood Stork, and American Robin are found farther north. Birders know that certain exotic species have made their home in tropical South Florida, including Spot-breasted Oriole and Red Junglefowl, both in the game.

The game doesn’t tell you what species to look for, so you’ll need to cover a lot of ground to find everything.

That geographic fidelity is the main tip for anyone looking to check off all the game’s birds: Find their habitat. The game doesn’t tell you what species to look for, so you’ll need to cover a lot of ground to find everything. Wading birds, shorebirds, and seabirds are common in the southern coastal part of the map. A number of smaller, open-country or forest birds are found up north. Owls come out at night. Birds in zoos and domestic farmyard birds count towards the 60, too, so don’t overlook those areas.

The most accurate aspect is the actual landscape. Having birded extensively across South Florida myself, the habitats are immediately recognizable, from the pebbly mangrove beaches at the Keys to the grassy swamps further north. The setting feels absolutely real, from the fonts on the roadside fruit stands to the roadkill alongside the highways. (I found dead wild boar and dead deer by the side of the road, and a Turkey Vulture somehow flew into a building I was in and died at my feet.) 

So, if you’re up for the kind of funhouse-mirror version of Florida that the game presents, you can have a blast, and adding birdwatching into the insane free-for-all makes for some delightful moments. This guy just wanted to look at a gull but ended up chucking a woman off a pier. I’ve made myself laugh out loud by screeching to a halt in the middle of busy highways or intersections and jumping out of my car to look for birds while motorist behind me scream and swear. I love it.

As I was dodging dirt-biking rednecks to look for species in a northerly forest, I couldn’t help but compare birding in GTA 6 to birding in Red Dead Redemption 2 (RDR2), the latest installment in Rockstar’s other huge franchise. Birds and birding are presented quite similarly in both games, including the binocular “study” mechanic and many of the same species, but the experiences are totally different. RDR2 was set in the late 1800s, and players rode on horseback or locomotive through a comparatively wide-open and untrammeled version of America. There was mayhem in that game, too—outlaw gangs and marauders—but there were many peaceful moments, too, when a player could look out across wild landscape with nothing but nature as a soundtrack.

It is, I have to admit, a realistic depiction of birding in South Florida.

Not so in GTA 6. There’s plenty of birdsong on the soundtrack, but it competes with an endless racket of car radios, revving engines, swearing pedestrians, and blaring horns. There’s garbage almost everywhere, lapping the shorelines and blowing along the streets. There seems to be no inch of landscape in GTA 6 that hasn’t been lessened by the touch of humans. It is, I have to admit, a realistic depiction of birding in South Florida.

Though GTA 6 provides what feels the most accurate version of modern birding ever put to screen, obsessing over the realism misses the point of having fun. Birders can, and should, visit the real South Florida to find the actual birds depicted in the game (and many more), but they can’t do it while also jumping over broken highway bridges on a stolen motorcycle. Birding is a bonus in GTA 6; mayhem is the draw.

I played for two hours after midnight, then about six straight hours Thursday before having to call it quits to write this column. In that time I found 50 of the 60 species listed in the “birds” compendium. I jumped back in this morning before work and found a huge chunk of species at a zoo (makes sense!) and on a farm, then found the 60th and final, an American Barn Owl, on a water tank in a town in the north of the map. Success!
