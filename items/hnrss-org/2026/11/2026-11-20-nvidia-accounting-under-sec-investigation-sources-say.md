Nvidia's worst week on record just got worse. Now the government is asking questions about its books.

Nvidia ([NVDA](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/NVDA/)) stock fell nearly 14% Friday, its worst day since the DeepSeek rout in January 2025 and its seventh straight loss. The drop came as [Reuters reported](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/us-sec-s-nvidia-probe-is-examining-potential-accounting-issues-sources-say-ce785ed3dd8ff627) that the Securities and Exchange Commission's probe of the chipmaker is examining potential accounting issues.

The Wall Street Journal reported Tuesday that the probe includes an examination of whether Nvidia's disclosures misled investors. Nvidia didn't respond to Bloomberg's request for comment on that report, and the SEC declined to comment.

The selling set records. Nvidia shed about $485 billion in market value Friday and nearly $900 billion this week, as its stock had its worst week since October 2008. That tops the $573 billion it lost last week, previously the biggest one-week drop for any company on record, according to Dow Jones Market Data.

Since its Oct. 6 record, Nvidia has lost about $2.7 trillion in market value, more than the $2.5 trillion that [The Kobeissi Letter](https://x.com/KobeissiLetter/status/2123808105062646216) called the biggest 45-day loss for any stock in history. The world's most valuable company six weeks ago now ranks fourth in the US, behind Apple ([AAPL](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AAPL/)), Microsoft ([MSFT](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/MSFT/)) and Alphabet ([GOOGL](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/GOOGL/)).

Nvidia's [latest quarterly report](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/the-bank-of-nvidias-tab-jumped-to-nearly-670-billion--and-its-customers-are-taking-longer-to-pay-chart-of-the-day-110000229.html) raised a few questions. The company said its 10-Q, the detailed quarterly filing, would come "in the coming weeks" instead of the same evening as usual, and the release mentioned an ongoing investigation by an independent board committee.

Nvidia booked about $104 billion of revenue through its Singapore unit in 2025, roughly half its sales, the Financial Times reported. The Journal said the disclosure questions include Singapore-based Megaspeed, an Nvidia customer US officials suspect of rerouting its chips to China.

Nvidia's customers are also taking longer to pay, with bills outstanding at 64 days of sales, the most since 2022. It's not clear which of these, if any, the SEC is focused on.

Washington wants answers fast. "I think we need answers in the next 10 days from the board and from the outside counsel," Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent said Tuesday, after Jensen Huang's exit as CEO.

The sell-off spread across [chip stocks](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/nvidia-amd-and-broadcom-lead-chip-stocks-lower-133658985.html), which had [their worst week since 2001](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/article/chips-are-crashing-like-its-2008-as-the-nasdaq-100-nears-a-bear-market-192805534.html), and the broader market.

Baird's Ted Mortonson, a technology desk sector strategist, saw more scrutiny coming even before Friday's report.

"Every single deal that Nvidia has done on circular finance is going to be put under the microscope," he said on Yahoo Finance's live show Thursday.
