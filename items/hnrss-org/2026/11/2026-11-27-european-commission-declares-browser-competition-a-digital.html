In comments to Politico regarding our open letter, the European Commission acknowledged the letter and noted that browsers are “effectively the entry point to the web” and one of the main ways publishers reach readers. They went on to say that “browser competition is one of the priorities of the Digital Markets Act”. The EU Commission also stated it is in talks to ensure rival browsers “can effectively compete” with Safari on iPhones and iPads.

The letter has garnered incredible support. We want to say a massive thank you to all the organisations, businesses and individuals that have stepped forward to defend the web's ability to compete fairly as an open platform for consumers and businesses alike.

What is particularly extraordinary is the sheer diversity of the coalition, ranging from human rights, consumer rights, software freedom advocacy organisations to Europe's major publishing associations, prominent startup organisations, independent browser vendors and technology companies.

The breadth of this support demonstrates that browser and web app competition is not simply an issue for browser vendors, but one that affects the entire digital economy and society more broadly.

The Commission Must Now Act

We welcome the Commission's recognition that browser competition is a priority under the DMA. However in the more than two and a half years after Apple's obligations came into effect, not a single browser vendor has been able to upgrade its existing EU users to its own browser engine.

The extraordinary breadth of this coalition demonstrates both the importance of the web as a competitive platform and the scale of the opportunity being denied to European consumers and businesses.

Apple cannot be allowed to indefinitely frustrate the objectives of the DMA through successive rounds of ineffective compliance. The Commission must now move beyond dialogue, commence formal enforcement proceedings and compel Apple to remove the remaining barriers to effective browser and web app competition.

The web has waited long enough.
