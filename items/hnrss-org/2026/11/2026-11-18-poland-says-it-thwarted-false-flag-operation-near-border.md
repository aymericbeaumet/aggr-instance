Russian intelligence services reportedly planned to carry out a sabotage operation on Polish territory near the border with Russia's Kaliningrad Oblast in late October or early November which they intended to be blamed on Ukraine.

**Source:** Polish newspaper [Rzeczpospolita](https://www.rp.pl/konflikty-zbrojne/art45490041-prowokacja-z-duza-liczba-ofiar-i-zrzuceniem-winy-na-ukraine-ustalilismy-co-powstrzymalo-rosje), as cited by European Pravda

**Details:** The newspaper cited senior sources with ties to Poland's military and security services who are familiar with an operation carried out over the past two weeks to foil the Russian plot.

Rzeczpospolita said Warsaw had been warned of the planned operation by partner intelligence services, and Polish intelligence analysts had also identified signs of the threat.

"We had information about the specific sites that were to be attacked. The Russian plan envisaged mass casualties," one of the newspaper's sources said.

Drawing parallels with the Gleiwitz incident, a German false-flag operation carried out on 31 August 1939, another source said: "It was supposed to be something similar to Gleiwitz. Of course, those who crossed the border would most likely have been shot, or evidence that they had come from Ukraine would have been found on them. The aim was to stop aid to Ukraine."

Polish intelligence services took action after Russian intelligence officers responsible for coordinating sabotage operations appeared on the Russian side of the border. Such operations are mainly planned and coordinated by Russia's military intelligence agency, the GRU.

Police forces in areas bordering Russia were reinforced by officers from other parts of Poland in late October for this reason. Border Guard posts also received reinforcements, and additional Internal Security Agency officers were deployed to the area. Special forces held exercises in the Warmian-Masurian region, where Poland's Territorial Defence Forces had also held exercises earlier.

"The aim was deterrence, but also to ensure the ability to take pre-emptive action," a source familiar with the details said.

Rzeczpospolita asked Tomasz Siemoniak, Poland's Minister-Coordinator of Special Services, whether the Russians had planned to carry out a terrorist attack in Poland at a location where large numbers of people might have been present.

"I don't know the answer to that question – you'd have to ask the Russians, and they don't usually admit to planning such actions. Even if we have such information, we don't share it," he replied.

Siemoniak added, however: "If we took certain measures, it was because we considered the threat to be real.

In my opinion, all the measures taken in this case were appropriate. The available evidence indicated that the threat had been assessed as real," the minister said.

**Background:**

-  In early November, a special operations headquarters was established at Poland's National Police Headquarters for an operation being conducted in the Warmian-Masurian and Podlaskie voivodeships. Security forces were preparing for possible acts of sabotage on the border with Kaliningrad Oblast.

-  On 3 November, Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk said that "[suspicious activity](https://www.pravda.com.ua/eng/news/2026/11/03/8061046/)" had been detected overnight on the Polish-Russian border.
