I assume you know about the P vs. NP problem. But what is this wacky thing Anthropic just posted?

You learned how to take a determinant in your linear algebra class:

det(123456789)=1⋅(5⋅9-6⋅8)-2⋅(4⋅9-6⋅7)+3⋅(4⋅8-5⋅7)=0

The naive way is fine for 3×3 matrices, but notice that you need to perform Θ(n!) arithmetic operations here. Exercise for the reader is to find the exact number of operations (hint: start with a recurrence relation).

Luckily, if you paid attention, you will remember there are faster ways to do this for big n×n matrices, like Gaussian elimination. If we stick only to adding and multiplying, we can still find a determinant in polynomial time (there are better ways, but this is an old, readable method).

A related problem to the determinant is finding the permanent. It’s like a determinant without any subtraction:

perm(123456789)=1⋅(5⋅9+6⋅8)+2⋅(4⋅9+6⋅7)+3⋅(4⋅8+5⋅7)=450

Unlike determinants, there is no known way to compute permanents in polynomial time. Since finding the permanent of a matrix of 0s and 1s is NP-hard (and in fact #P-complete), it would be pretty important if there were a way.
