Aged mice engineered to lack granzyme K have improved heart and liver function and less kidney damage.

![Light micrograph of liver cirrhosis, showing irregular red tissue regions separated by a network of blue branching bands.](https://media.nature.com/w767/magazine-assets/d41586-026-03638-3/d41586-026-03638-3_53962206.jpg)

Age-related stiffening and scarring of liver tissue (pictured) declined in mice that were bioengineered to lack a certain enzyme. Credit: Ziad M. El-Zaatari/Science Photo Library

Researchers have found an enzyme that drives ageing in the body’s tissues [1](https://www.nature.com/articles/d41586-026-03638-3#ref-CR1). Drugs that reduce the levels of this protein might one day combat the effects of ageing without impairing the body’s immune defences.
