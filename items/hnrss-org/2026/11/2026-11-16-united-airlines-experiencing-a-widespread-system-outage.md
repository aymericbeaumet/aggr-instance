A widespread, yet short-lived, system outage at United Airlines on Monday delayed some outbound flights at US airports, the airline told CNN in a statement, coming on the heels of a [rare FAA ground stop](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/14/politics/faa-ground-stop-what-we-know) that caused [massive disruption](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/13/us/live-news/flights-ground-stop-faa).

The outage lasted about 30 minutes, with fewer than 150 flights impacted, United Airlines spokesperson Leslie Scott told CNN.

United [acknowledged](https://x.com/united/status/2122449364819886277) the issue just before 5 p.m. ET, saying the technology outage was impacting departing flights, and an hour later the airline announced it had been resolved.

“While some flights were delayed, there was no impact on flights in progress, or on United Express flights,” United Airlines said in a statement.

During the incident, the FAA said United was experiencing a technology outage on its end and that “it’s not an FAA equipment issue.”

United said there is no indication that the outage was a cybersecurity-related issue.

The technology outage affected access to multiple applications used by flight crews, according to an internal company alert obtained by CNN.

The airline instructed pilots not to log out of several systems, warning they may be unable to sign back in. The affected applications included flight planning, weather briefing and crew management tools.

Pilots unable to access their flight plans were being directed to contact dispatchers to have the documents emailed to them.

The memo did not disclose the cause of the outage.

Air traffic control audio captured by [ATC.com](http://ATC.com) shows the moment the outage occurred.

“Hey, we’re uh, still waiting on our final weights,” a pilot on United Flight 1728 in El Paso, heading to Denver, said to a controller. “We just contacted ops and apparently there’s a company-wide system outage on it so might be a while.”

The controller responds back, “United 1728, roger, so you don’t have any idea when you think you’ll be able to leave?”

“No ma’am,” the pilot responds.

The industry had largely returned to normal operations after the nationwide ground stop was lifted just after 8 p.m. ET on Friday, though airlines have still worked to reposition [planes, crews and luggage](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/14/us/live-news/us-ground-stop-flights?post-id=cmwgq5lyx00003b6utawcyem5) as they grappled with the weekend’s [score of cancellations and delays](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/14/us/live-news/us-ground-stop-flights?post-id=cmwgqcoig0000356vzunestfq).

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy held a 45-minute news conference Saturday morning to address the FAA ground stop, and apologized to travelers as he said he was still working to determine what exactly went wrong.

Duffy told reporters that [upgrade work on an old telecommunication system](https://www.cnn.com/2026/11/14/politics/faa-ground-stop-what-we-know) could be a cause for Friday’s nationwide ground stop.

In at least one remote FAA location, Duffy said contractor L3Harris was doing routine maintenance to switch telecommunication lines from an old copper wire to fiber, which he described as “straightforward” work that has been happening across the country for decades.

As United Airlines indicated for Monday’s system outage, Friday’s ground stop didn’t appear to have been caused by a cyber attack or any “nefarious” activity, Duffy stressed at the news conference.

“A lot of questions came in yesterday. ‘Was this a, a cyber attack? Did some foreign nation attack this network?’ And the answer to that is we don’t believe so,” Duffy said.
