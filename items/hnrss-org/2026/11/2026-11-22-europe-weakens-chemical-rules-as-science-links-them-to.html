Stéphane Foucart

A report by the World Health Organization (WHO) and the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) reveals that, for just seven of the best-known molecules or groups of substances, the burden of health damage amounts to €1 trillion per year, highlights Le Monde columnist Stéphane Foucart.

If you are ill, it is probably your fault. If you have breast cancer, diabetes, a thyroid or immune system disorder, endometriosis, polycystic ovary syndrome, or if you are infertile, it is because you did not lead your life properly. You likely indulged in tobacco, alcohol, a sedentary lifestyle, poor diet, or other vices. And if not, perhaps the problem lies in your DNA, or you were simply unlucky. And do not be ungrateful: Medical advances are significant, and research is moving forward every day.

Repeated tirelessly, these talking points have ended up shaping public health policy. The point here is not to argue that tobacco and alcohol consumption (and other risky behaviors) are not major scourges, but rather to counter that the one-sided discourse on individual responsibility ends up naturalizing disease and absolving decision-makers of responsibility. The World Health Organization (WHO) and the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) have just shattered this rhetoric.

On Wednesday, November 18, the two organizations published a long-awaited expert report. It provides the most up-to-date scientific assessment of the harm caused by endocrine disruptors: chemical substances (such as pesticides, plasticizers, solvents, and PFAS, or "forever chemicals") to which everyone is exposed without their consent, whether through food, air, water, or the home environment. The previous assessment dated back to 2012, and in the intervening 14 years, the accumulation of new data has led to major advances, such as the ability to estimate the number of cases of various diseases and disorders attributable to the best-studied substances.

Pesticides? According to the WHO-UNEP report, prenatal exposure (as of 2010) to a single family of these products (organophosphates) was, for example, responsible for more than 59,300 cases of intellectual disability per year in European Union (EU) countries. Due to maternal exposure to just this one category of pesticides, a European child born in 2010 lost, on average, about 2.5 IQ points, according to the panel of researchers brought together by WHO and UNEP.
