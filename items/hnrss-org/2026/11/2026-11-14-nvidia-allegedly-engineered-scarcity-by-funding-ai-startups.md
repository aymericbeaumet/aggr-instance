Following the [departure of now-former NVIDIA CEO, Jensen Huang](https://www.techpowerup.com/354732/nvidia-co-founder-and-ceo-jensen-huang-officially-resigns), a new report by [The Wall Street Journal](https://www.wsj.com/tech/jensen-huang-colette-kress-nvidia-ceo-eb289097?) (via [Wccftech](https://wccftech.com/jensen-huang-financed-ai-startups-absorb-excess-silicon/)) has emerged alleging that NVIDIA artificially inflated the ongoing GPU and memory shortage by investing in AI startups that would go on to buy NVIDIA GPUs and build data centers. The reason behind this was to maintain the perception of ongoing high demand and to move an oversupply of semiconductors from TSMC.

The WSJ report cites an August meeting between Huang and his senior finance team, including NVIDIA's CFO and acting CEO, Colette Kress, during which Kress pushed for a reduction in circular financing agreements, in response to which Huang allegedly lay into Kress, calling her a "deer in the headlights," after she had pushed to scale back [NVIDIA's $250 billion deal with OpenAI](https://www.techpowerup.com/351120/nvidia-and-openai-reportedly-discuss-usd-250-billion-deal-for-10-gigawatt-ohio-data-center). Jensen's push for further investment into small-scale cloud providers was seemingly in response to the changing tide that saw AI hyperscalers move away from off-the-shelf components in favor of custom solutions.

[![](https://tpucdn.com/img/ooCln2q3jkKNGHen_thm.jpg)](https://www.techpowerup.com/img/ooCln2q3jkKNGHen.jpg)

The situation described in the report saw NVIDIA on the hook for an "avalanche" of GPUs from TSMC, for which it would have no immediate buyers. Part of the issue highlighted by Kress was also that these small-scale cloud providers NVIDIA was investing in could not afford to buy NVIDIA GPUs, meaning they, too, could be a financial dead end.
