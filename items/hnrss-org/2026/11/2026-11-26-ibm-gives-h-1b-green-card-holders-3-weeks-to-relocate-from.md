[IBM](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/topic/ibm) has given some of its US-based employees holding H-1B visas and green cards three weeks to decide whether to relocate to another country or leave the company, as the technology giant ends immigration sponsorship for most positions in its consulting division.

The move could affect foreign professionals who depend on employer sponsorship to maintain their US immigration status and pursue permanent residency. IBM has not disclosed the number of employees who will be affected.

## Employees have three weeks to decide

According to a report by Rest of World, employees have been given three weeks to decide whether to accept positions outside the US or leave the company. Those who decline overseas roles will be offered three months' severance.

Some employees were informed of the decision during one-on-one meetings with their managers, which were described as career discussions.

Employees choosing to leave rather than relocate have been given December 31, 2026, as their last working day. The decision applies to [IBM Consulting](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/topic/ibm-consulting), the company's largest division, which employs more than 150,000 people globally.

## IBM ends visa sponsorship for most consulting roles

IBM announced earlier this month that it would discontinue US visa and [green card sponsorship](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/topic/green-card-sponsorship) for the majority of positions in its consulting division. The decision was first reported by Bloomberg on November 10, citing an internal company document.

An IBM spokesperson confirmed the development, saying the company's participation in high-skilled visa programmes had been declining for several years.

"IBM Consulting participates in US high-skilled visa programs to address skill shortages," the spokesperson said in a statement.

"That participation has been declining for a number of years, and as skill availability and client need have evolved, we have opted to discontinue visa and green card sponsorship for the majority of such roles in IBM Consulting," the spokesperson added.

The change is limited to most positions in IBM Consulting and does not mean IBM is ending visa and green card sponsorship across all its business units.

## India accounts for most overseas job openings

India could be the main destination for employees choosing to relocate. According to Rest of World, IBM's careers portal listed 1,278 consulting vacancies outside the US on November 19. Of these, 1,125 were in India, 23 in Singapore and 18 in the UK.

The company also listed 89 openings in the US.

IBM Consulting employs more than 150,000 people worldwide. However, the company has not disclosed the geographical distribution of its workforce, making it difficult to estimate how many US-based employees could be affected.

The overseas vacancies could provide employees an option to continue working for IBM without remaining in the US. However, the company has not disclosed how many affected employees will be offered transfers or the terms of those positions.
