Often in life, your body is the one steering you. And sometimes, to get to do the thing you want, you can’t just convince yourself with reason or arguments, or will yourself, or do the exercises to get there - you need to learn the magic formula, the secret words, to trick the body into acting in a way that gets you the outcome you want.

As a kid, spending time on the internet forums for bands I loved, I kept hearing about some magical book called [The Easy Way to Stop Smoking by Allen Carr,](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/61359664-the-easy-way-to-stop-smoking) that cured people of their smoking addiction. Middle-aged man after middle-aged man would praise the miracles of this book - not by the patch, not by gum, not by sheer will, only by reading this book could they quit smoking - and not only that, everyone they had given the book to had the same result.

As a 12 year old, unable to find out what this book actually said, I spent hours thinking about this - trying to figure out what secret trick the book could be sharing that was so powerful. \[to clarify, I have never smoked a cigarette in my life\]

So after countless nights thinking through this, this is what I came up with: you want to smoke because the craving feels bad, not because you actually want the cigarette. So you need to find a way to tame the cravings, and each time you don’t smoke, your cravings become smaller and smaller. Maybe you could do this by saying to yourself - this is just a craving and nothing to do with the goodness of smoking, and while the craving really sucks, the thing I’m trying to satisfy is just a craving. Each time I don’t smoke, I win this battle, and the craving will be smaller. And so long as I can go to war and bring all my focus to defeating this craving in this one battle, eventually each craving will become smaller until they all go away.

I forgot all about this book, and cigarettes, and whether or not I was right - until one day I realized I had become addicted to sugar and was experiencing lots of cravings to eat something sweet after every meal. I realized I could finally try this technique for the first time, and shortly after doing so, I overcame all the cravings. (I later learned that the book basically had the same idea as me, that there is a little “craving” monster inside you that dies when you stop feeding it - so I give 12 year old Daniel a lot of credit for this one.)

Something similar happened when Scott Alexander reviewed [The Body Keeps the Score](https://slatestarcodex.com/2019/11/12/book-review-the-body-keeps-the-score/\)), a book about PTSD and the theory that trauma gets stuck in the body. Even though Scott was unimpressed by the contents of the book, he retells recommending it to a severely traumatized patient who hadn’t gotten better after years of conventional treatment, and the next week the patient came back saying they were cured. Shockingly, the patient hadn’t tried any of the techniques in the book. Reading it just helped them find some “indescribable mental motion” that made the problem go away. Scott had no idea what to make of this.

The theory seems to be that the pain starts with some discomfort, which causes you to think something is wrong, which causes you to focus on the area and tense up, which convinces the body to focus on and tense this area further, until it materializes into actual awful and generally incurable pain. But once you convince yourself that there is nothing structurally wrong with your body, you can break this cycle and the pain goes away. It’s the same as the smoking book. No matter how badly one wants something, no matter how much they try, or how much “rehab” they do, they cannot get the body and mind aligned until they learn the magic formula to steer the body.

If I lie in bed at night and tell myself I want to fall asleep in twenty minutes, I can’t. But if I tell myself I need to lie in bed scanning how my body feels, and noticing any time my mind starts to wander, for twenty minutes as timed by my watch, I will be asleep before the twenty minutes are up - and I can do this basically on command, but funny enough, only if my watch is actually timing me.

When I feel even mildly stressed, or angry about something, I feel my body as being heavy. But if I tell myself to smile and imagine my body being filled with air like a balloon - I not only immediately feel physically lighter, but I also feel much more relaxed and the tension evaporates. It’s as if my body’s weight is what determines my mood, and I can manipulate my body’s “weight” just by thinking about the balloons. I can’t just tell myself to be happier or less stressed, I need to think about the balloons. I need to trick my body in a way that it understands, so the body can lead the mind to the right place.

Often in life, we think about things through facts and logic and abstract arguments. But knowing what we should do, and all the reasons for it, is not enough to get the body to do it. For many things, we can’t just tell ourselves to be stronger, or to be tougher, and get the body to do the thing we want, and sometimes even therapy or rehab can’t get us there. Sometimes we need to find a way to steer the body, and the body only listens when you say the magic words. I like these examples, not only because they are all actually powerful and useful, but also because it feels like I am whispering the secrets of the universe - that through saying the magic words, I am being granted special root access to my own programming.

And then I think about how many other things like this there are in life - whether through ritual, or art, or doing something in some particular way, like my watch actually timing me, we get ourselves to reach an outcome that otherwise, no matter how much we want to get to, or how much we try, is closed off to us. Because our bodies not our minds govern so many of our outcomes - and our body is not persuaded by facts and logic. I am sure there are many more examples like those I shared here, if you have one, please share it.
