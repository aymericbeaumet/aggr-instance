From a pocket detector to a shared map

Everything on the map is public and free to browse. An account is only needed to publish.

  1. Measure

    A RadiaCode detector in your pocket logs dose rate and location every second, on a walk, a road trip or a flight.

  2. Share a track

    Publish the recording from the RadiaCode app. Your track joins the map, anonymously if you prefer, and its points feed the hexagon layer everyone sees.

  3. Explore and describe

    Zoom from country coverage down to single measurements. Found something? Outline it as an area, add photos and context, and discuss it with others.

Privacy and data quality

Sharing measurements should never mean sharing where you live. The full details are in the Privacy Policy.

Your data, your call

  • Private until you publish

    Tracks stay private by default. Making one public takes a separate, explicit consent.

  • Publish anonymously

    Public tracks can hide who recorded them while their measurements still count on the map. Your profile shows a nickname, never your Google or Apple name.

  • A 24-hour delay

    Public tracks are held back for a day before anyone else sees them, so nobody can follow you in real time.

  • Export or delete, any time

    Request a copy of your data or delete your account from your profile. Accounts inactive for 36 months are removed, with a warning first.

Keeping the map honest

  • Precise positions only

    Measurements with poor GPS accuracy, worse than 15 metres, are left off the map.

  • Sensitive places protected

    Measurements inside protected locations are removed from public view.

  • Reviewed areas

    New areas and edits to existing ones are reviewed by moderators before they go public.

  • Moderated conversations

    Comments pass automatic moderation checks, and signed-in users can report any track, area or comment.

  • No raw coordinate dumps

    Public interfaces never hand out raw GPS tracks, and scraping is rate-limited and blocked.
