In 2006, two brothers launched a website using dial-up modems and a battered book on writing HTML4, named it after a character who woke up as a giant insect, and hoped someone might find it useful.

Twenty years and 766,615,348 file conversions later, apparently someone did.

Some Ridiculous Numbers

Back when we launched Zamzar in 2006 we proudly offered a whopping 150 different conversion types. We now offer almost 15x that 👀, with 2,128 conversions supported in 2026. Over 550 million people have used Zamzar since launch, converting over 766 million files. We now process over 1 million API requests per day.

Big numbers are great (they keep our resident stats-man Mike happy) but more important than features and numbers are the people that find and use Zamzar every day. Last year alone, we had:

  • One visitor from the remote pacific island of Tokelau – with a population of 1,499 it is the fourth smallest sovereign state in the world.
  • Three from Svalbard – a remote Norwegian archipelago which bans cats and has more snowmobiles than people.
  • 38 from Antarctica 🥶 – 🙌 to the 1,000 scientists who stay there over Winter (hope the vending machines are well stocked).
  • 41 from the Vatican – h.t. to Leo 🕍 🕊️ 🙏

Twenty years of unexpected adventures

It turns out that running a file conversion website can lead you to some pretty strange places.

We’ve helped the inventor of PowerPoint recover long-lost presentations (yes, really), supported internet pioneer Vint Cerf in his efforts to preserve our digital history, and partnered with Xerox to help their printers turn documents into spoken word audio.

We’ve been asked to convert sewing machine patterns (we couldn’t), resurrect ancient eFax files (we could), and solve more obscure file format mysteries than we ever imagined existed.

Along the way, we’ve appeared on the BBC’s Click, received a cease-and-desist letter from Google for letting people download YouTube videos, and somehow ended up sponsoring a team of Scottish curling champions. That last one definitely wasn’t in the original business plan.

Still small, still independent

Twenty years on, we’re still a small, proudly independent UK company. No venture capital, no unicorn valuations, and no plans to pivot to an AI-powered blockchain for converting your Gran’s Word documents. We’ve watched countless technology trends come and go – social media, Web 2.0, smartphones, blockchain, wearable tech, NFT’s, the metaverse and more recently the arrival of Al Pacino, sorry AI.. Throughout it all people still need to convert their files.

Being independent has also given us the freedom to do things our own way. We’ve supported more than 60 charities, schools and good causes, donating a portion of our profits to organisations doing far more important work than converting PDFs.

The people behind the files

Behind every one of these 766 million files is a real person with a real problem. Like Robin, who thought 36 pages of work had been lost to “gibberish”, until Zamzar brought them back to life. Or Simha, who discovered Zamzar through a friend in Israel more than a decade ago, and still uses us today from Mexico.

It’s stories like these that remind us why we started – making someone’s day a little easier, one file at a time.
