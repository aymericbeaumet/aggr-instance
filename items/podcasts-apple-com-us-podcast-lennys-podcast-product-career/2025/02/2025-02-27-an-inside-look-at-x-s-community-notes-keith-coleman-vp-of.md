**Keith Coleman** (VP of product) and **Jay Baxter** (founding ML engineer), the minds behind Community Notes, reveal how a small, scrappy team inside Twitter/X built the most trusted crowdsourced information system on the internet—one that’s changing the way we understand truth online. What you’ll learn:

1. **How Community Notes actually works** —a deep dive into the groundbreaking algorithm that rewards “bridging agreement” instead of majority rule

2. **The seemingly crazy yet brilliant way this idea survived multiple CEO changes** —from Jack to Parag to Elon

3. **How this project started with a dumpster fire GIF (literally)** —the untold backstory of its early launch

4. **The secret to running ultra-fast, high-impact product teams** —no OKRs, no Jira; just one Google Doc

5. **What Meta’s adoption of Community Notes means for the future of online (mis)information** —why this open source system is becoming the industry standard

[www.youtube.com](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8dgyqYHLcCI)

**Some takeaways:**

1. Key principles that enabled Community Notes’ success:

   1. Universal access: Random selection of contributors rather than curated experts

   2. Complete transparency: Open source code and data

   3. Focus on bridging divides: Show notes that people across the political spectrum find helpful

   4. Prove it works: Demonstrate success at each stage before expanding

   5. Let the people decide: No “god mode” or company override of community decisions
2. The system’s effectiveness stems from focusing on bridging-based agreement:

   1. Notes only appear when rated helpful by people who typically disagree

   2. About 8% of proposed notes meet the quality threshold

   3. Notes can reduce engagement with misleading content by 50% to 60%

   4. Authors are 80% more likely to delete posts after receiving a note
3. Small, focused teams can have outsize impact when structured correctly:

   1. Single clear owner/decision-maker

   2. 100% dedicated team members

   3. Freedom from standard corporate processes

   4. Start with minimal necessary roles (ML, frontend, backend, design, research)

   5. Use lightweight tools (e.g. a single Google Doc vs. complex project management systems)
4. Building trust through radical transparency:

   1. All code and data is public and downloadable

   2. External researchers can replicate and verify results

   3. Community members can propose algorithm improvements

   4. Regular public updates on system changes

   5. No hidden controls or override mechanisms
