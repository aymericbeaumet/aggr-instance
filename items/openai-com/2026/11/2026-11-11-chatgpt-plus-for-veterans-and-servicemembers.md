## 20,000 veterans are using ChatGPT to build what comes next

In the past year, more than 20,000 transitioning servicemembers and veterans have claimed a year of [ChatGPT Plus at no cost](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-for-veterans/) after being offered free access by OpenAI. That milestone gives us a chance to look at how veterans are using AI to prepare for civilian careers and to figure out what comes next for themselves and their families.

The effort started with veterans who work here at OpenAI, people who use these tools every day and know firsthand how challenging the transition can be. Beyond the free access, OpenAI Academy and our community partners have hosted more than a dozen trainings for nearly 1,000 members of the military community, [with seven more](https://academy.openai.com/home/events) scheduled before the year ends.

## Preparing for civilian careers

Leaving the military often means stepping into an unfamiliar job market and working out how years of service translate into a civilian role. Many veterans are turning to ChatGPT to organize their job search, rehearse for interviews, and explore paths they hadn’t considered.

## Learning to use ChatGPT, together

Not everyone arrives confident about using AI tools, which is why OpenAI Academy gives veterans, transitioning servicemembers, and military spouses a place to learn alongside people who understand what they’re going through.

OpenAI recently wrapped our first six-event Academy series with [Veterans Forge](https://veteransforge.org/), focused on helping veterans and military families use ChatGPT to work toward their goals. That pilot, together with workshops alongside AI Collective Hampton Roads and NPower, is shaping what we offer veterans next.

At one [workshop in Reston, Virginia](https://academy.openai.com/en/public/events/openai-academy-for-veterans-w8zz8dfrts), nearly [100 veterans and active-duty servicemembers](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/erinrosemarie_veterans-ai-aicommunity-activity-7440044135958384640-j7S-) explored ways to put AI to work. Participant [Ryan Kaono-Wedemeyer](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ryan-kaono-wedemeyer) learned to start by writing out a detailed account of his military experience, then asking AI to help shape it into a civilian résumé.

Instructor [Erin Marie](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/erinrosemarie_veterans-ai-aicommunity-activity-7440044135958384640-j7S-) helped participants build custom GPTs to support their transitions, and watched people who walked in skeptical begin finding ways the tools could genuinely help them.

In Louisville, Kentucky, [more than 80 Legionnaires](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/veterans-forge_theamericanlegion-veteransforge-veterans-activity-7499820737151541248-PqzY) attended three AI training sessions at the American Legion’s national convention. For those who can’t make it to an event, our [online veterans collection](https://academy.openai.com/public/collections/veterans) and [100 chats for veterans](https://chatgpt.com/use-cases/veterans) offer examples to try on your own.
