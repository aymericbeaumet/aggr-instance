The risks of “one size fits all” AI

Today’s AI landscape is dominated by closed, opaque offerings with limited flexibility, encouraging a “one size fits all” approach to AI. As public institutions look for solutions to help them implement AI strategies, those offerings come with significant limitations and risks.

  • Closed and inflexible
    Offerings from the dominant vendors are largely proprietary, black-box offerings that are difficult or impossible to tailor and customize, severely limiting transparency, flexibility, and effectiveness while leaving countries captive to the priorities, architectures, and approaches of outside vendors.
  • Dependence on dominant vendors
    A small number of dominant providers currently provide the lion’s share of AI technology and expertise, sapping local innovation, creating monopolistic vendor lock-in, and transferring significant economic value to foreign players.
  • Subject to geopolitical risks
    Dependence on vendors and technologies that are subject to control and regulation by superpowers creates significant risk and uncertainty as AI increasingly becomes a strategic lever in trade, regulatory, and competitive battles.
  • Lack of control
    Dependence on foreign technology can severely limit the ability to ensure that data transfer, processing, and governance are aligned with national requirements for safety, privacy, and protection.
  • AI diversity
    Reliance on AI models, algorithms, and applications tuned and trained by a few dominant foreign vendors can easily result in AI that does not serves the full range of languages, cultures, norms, and perspectives globally.

To sum it up, "one size fits all" AI offerings cannot meet the tactical nor strategic needs of regions and countries. Understanding local languages, incorporating local context, and aligning with unique priorities in each region and country requires AI solutions that are designed and tailored to fit the specific use cases and requirements of those regions, countries and their people. Not only that, but “one size fits all” AI offerings take control of the impact of AI on their future out of the hands of countries and their citizens.
